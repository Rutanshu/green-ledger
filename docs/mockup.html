If you're new to this

What's a “scope”?

Scope 1 — fuel you burn yourself. Generators, boilers, your own vehicles, refrigerant that leaks out.

Scope 2 — energy you buy. Electricity, steam, heating, cooling.

Scope 3 — everything else in your value chain: what you buy, how it's shipped, staff travel, waste, what happens to your products. Usually the biggest and the hardest.

What's an emission factor?

A published number that converts an activity into emissions. One litre of diesel becomes 2.68 kg of CO₂e.

You don't invent them — you cite them. DEFRA, EPA, IEA and national grid operators publish them, and they change every year. Green Ledger stores which version you used on each record, so old reports never shift under you.

Why can't I edit last year?

Because someone relied on that report. If the numbers could quietly change, the report would be worthless.

Corrections to a locked period become restatements: the old figure and the new one both appear, with a reason. Disclosed rather than hidden — which is what CSRD requires anyway.

What will my auditor ask for?

The audit pack. One spreadsheet row per emission record with every input that produced it — quantity, unit, factor value, factor source and version, GWP, share, and the result.

They can recompute your entire inventory in Excel without logging in. Export it any time from Reports.

Do I need all 15 Scope 3 categories?

You need to address all 15. You don't need data for all 15.

Mark the ones that don't apply as not material with a reason. That reason appears in the report — which satisfies the requirement to explain the omission, instead of leaving a silent gap.

The one rule

Every number Green Ledger reports can be reproduced from stored inputs — forever. A report generated today produces identical figures in 2035, after every factor in the system has been superseded. Everything else in the product exists to serve that.

How a label is chosen

Most specific wins. First match going down this list is what renders.

1 On one factor mapping — “Generator fuel”
2 On one question
3 On one questionnaire template
4 On a site type — “Building heating” for offices
5 Across your organisation — “Fixed fuel burning”
6 Green Ledger's default — “Stationary combustion”

What you can and can't extend

Some lists are yours; some are defined by the standard

✓ Open Site types, asset types, materials, document types, intensity denominators — rename and add your own
⚠ Closed Scopes, the 15 Scope 3 categories, methods, data-quality levels — rename only

Closed lists come from the GHG Protocol and ESRS. Adding to them would break the report mapping, so the button says why instead of silently doing nothing.

The pencil is everywhere. An admin sees a faint ✎ beside any label anywhere in the app — a section heading, a dropdown option, a chart legend. Clicking it opens this editor already scoped to where they clicked. Renaming happens in context, not by hunting through settings.
